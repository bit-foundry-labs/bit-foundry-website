CHAPTER 03 · LESSON

Transport: delivery between processes

Transport uses ports to deliver data to the right process. TCP adds reliability, flow control, and congestion control; UDP keeps the service lightweight.

3.1–3.2 · SERVICE

Ports sort the traffic

Multiplexing gathers data from application sockets. Demultiplexing uses port numbers to pass each segment to the correct process.

Transport multiplexes data from application sockets and demultiplexes segments to the right process. Port numbers identify endpoints; TCP also uses connection state, while UDP uses a simpler endpoint mapping.

In practice: A laptop can run a browser, call, and DNS client at once because the OS separates their traffic.

3.3 · UDP

Connectionless transport

UDP sends independent segments without a handshake or built-in retransmission. Its checksum helps detect errors in a segment.

UDP provides ports and a checksum without a handshake, retransmission, or built-in congestion control. It fits applications that need low overhead and can manage timing or loss themselves.

In practice: A real-time app may skip a late audio packet instead of retransmitting it after it is no longer useful.

3.4–3.5 · RELIABILITY

TCP repairs loss

Sequence numbers, acknowledgments, timers, and retransmissions let TCP provide reliable, in-order delivery over IP.

Reliable transfer uses sequence numbers, acknowledgments, checksums, and timers to detect loss and restore in-order delivery. Go-Back-N repeats a missing packet and later unacknowledged packets; Selective Repeat repeats only missing packets and buffers later arrivals.

In practice: A sending window keeps multiple packets in flight while tracking what the receiver confirmed.

3.5–3.7 · CONTROL

Two different limits

Flow control keeps a fast sender from overwhelming the receiver. Congestion control responds to load inside the network.

Flow control protects a receiver’s buffer; congestion control responds to load inside the network. TCP estimates round-trip time for timeouts and adjusts its sending rate when it detects congestion.

In practice: A sender may be limited by a slow receiver, a congested path, or both; these controls solve different problems.

Reliable transfer: Go-Back-N retransmits the missing packet and later unacknowledged packets. Selective Repeat retransmits only missing packets and buffers out-of-order arrivals.
