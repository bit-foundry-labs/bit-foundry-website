CHAPTER 07 · GUIDED LESSONS

Wireless and Mobile Networks

How networks handle radio links and moving devices. Open any module for a five-page lesson with an explanation, a worked example, guided practice, and a checkpoint.

7.1 · MODULE

Wireless link behavior

Radio signals face attenuation, interference, and multipath; wireless links adapt to changing conditions.

Wireless links share spectrum and face attenuation, interference, and multipath fading. Signal quality changes with distance and obstacles; protocols adapt rates and use error control.

In practice: Moving a laptop behind a wall can reduce signal quality even when its IP address is unchanged.

7.2 · MODULE

Wi-Fi and local wireless

802.11 uses association and channel access rules to connect stations through an access point.

802.11 networks use stations, access points, and a distribution system. Wi-Fi stations contend for the channel and use acknowledgments because wireless collisions are difficult to detect while sending.

In practice: A busy access point can increase delay as connected devices compete for airtime.

7.3 · MODULE

Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Zigbee

Wireless LANs and personal-area networks use different link rules; Bluetooth and Zigbee trade range, rate, and power for nearby devices.

Wi-Fi frames carry addresses for wireless delivery and bridging; advanced 802.11 features improve throughput. Bluetooth and Zigbee support personal-area uses with different range, power, and rate tradeoffs.

In practice: A wearable may use Bluetooth to reach a phone, which forwards data over Wi-Fi or cellular.

7.4 · MODULE

Cellular networks and mobility

Cellular radio access, carrier-core services, and handoff keep a device connected as its point of attachment changes.

Cellular networks combine radio access nodes with a core that authenticates devices, assigns service, and connects traffic to the Internet. LTE organizes communication around base stations and packet-core functions.

In practice: The radio link ends at a base station; the carrier core manages the device’s session and traffic.
