PIXEL ARCHIVE / PLAY

Gaming history

How games moved from research labs into arcades, living rooms and pockets.

1958

Tennis for Two

Physicist William Higinbotham built a tennis simulation for an oscilloscope at Brookhaven National Laboratory. It made a scientific display playable.

1972

Pong hits the arcade

Atari's Pong helped turn arcade video games into a mainstream business. Its simple paddle-and-ball rules made the screen legible at a glance.

1977

Cartridges change the console

Atari introduced the Video Computer System. Interchangeable cartridges meant one console could become many different games.

1978

Space Invaders arrives in the US

Taito's arcade shooter brought waves of pixel aliens to US arcades and later became an early hit on the Atari VCS.

1985

NES launches in North America

Nintendo's home console brought tightly designed game worlds into millions of living rooms and helped revive the US console market.

1989

Game Boy makes play portable

Nintendo's handheld showed how a small, efficient device could support long-lasting play away from the television.
